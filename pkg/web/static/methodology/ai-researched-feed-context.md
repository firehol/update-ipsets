# AI-Researched Feed Context

Feed pages may include researched context about the feed maintainer, how
entries are listed and removed, upstream-stated update cadence, detection
method, intended use, redistribution terms, current status, and community
signals.

This section is descriptive. It explains what the maintainer or public sources
say about the feed. It is not a live verdict about the feed's quality and it is
not used by the update engine to decide whether an IP belongs in the list.

## How to read it

- **The story** is the long-form opening of the page: who publishes the feed,
  why it exists, what kinds of IPs it lists, and the intended audience.
- **Method** describes how the feed is built — primary detection method, whether
  it is an original first-party feed or derived from another source, and the
  maintainer-stated update cadence.
- **How IPs get on and off this list** describes the public listing, unlisting,
  and removal-request process the maintainer documents. If no clear process
  exists, the page says that instead of inventing one.
- **Reputation and community signals** are historical public observations at the
  bottom of the page. They may no longer describe the feed's current state.
- **Sources consulted** is the folded list of public materials the research
  drew on, with the date research was last refreshed. Open it to verify
  individual claims or to follow the research yourself.

## Strengths

- Gives users context that raw IP counts cannot provide.
- Keeps maintainer statements, observed behavior, and community history separate.
- Makes source material visible so readers can inspect the evidence directly.
- Helps users avoid treating all feeds as interchangeable blocklists.

## Limits

- Research can become stale when maintainers change their process or website.
- Some feeds publish little or no documentation, so unknown fields are expected.
- Community complaints can be real but old, fixed, or specific to one deployment.
- The research does not prove that every listed IP is correct.
- The research does not override the feed's current observed health, freshness,
  or redistribution policy.

When the researched context and observed behavior disagree, treat that as a
reason to inspect both signals. The feed page keeps them separate on purpose.
